# Sector groupings

What this covers: ONE's sector definitions by CRS purpose code and where they depart from the raw CRS structure.

## Concepts and definitions

CRS purpose codes are the OECD's classification of ODA activities. Each CRS activity carries a five-digit purpose code for its primary development objective, and the three-digit DAC sector codes group these purpose codes. Sector groupings aggregate them into broader categories for analysis.

ONE's usual sector groupings are below, built from three-digit DAC sector codes. A few CRS records carry only a heading-level code, such as 100 or 200, which falls outside every sector here, so those records count as unallocated.

| Education | 110 (111 to 114) |
| Health | 120 (121 to 123) + 130 |
| Water supply and sanitation | 140 |
| Government and civil society | 151 |
| Conflict, peace and security | 152 |
| Social infrastructure, protection and services | 160 |
| Transport, storage and communications | 210 + 220 |
| Energy | 230 (231 to 236) |
| Banking, financial services and business | 240 + 250 |
| Agriculture, forestry and fishing | 310 (311 to 313) |
| Industry, mining and construction | 320 (321 to 323) |
| Trade policies and regulations (incl. tourism) | 330 (331 + 332) |

These groupings are ONE's analytical convention. The OECD does not define them.

Health: ONE's health sector combines DAC sector groups 120 (health) and 130 (population policies and programmes, and reproductive health). Some analyses use 120 alone, which gives a smaller figure.

Users and other organisations often define sectors differently. A non-default definition changes the figures, so comparing results across definitions needs the difference stated.

## Pitfalls

- A sector definition that changes between calculations, such as 120 for one figure and 120 + 130 for another, makes the figures incomparable.
- Presenting ONE's groupings as official OECD definitions misstates their source.
- A sub-sector share, such as malaria within health, is calculated against the parent sector total, so dividing by total ODA gives a different and smaller figure.

## What makes a result verifiable

A sector result is verifiable when it lists the CRS codes that define each sector, notes any difference from ONE's usual grouping and the reason, and names the purpose codes and parent-sector denominator for any sub-sector share.
